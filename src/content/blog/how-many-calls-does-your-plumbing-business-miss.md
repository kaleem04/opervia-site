Ask a plumbing owner how many calls they miss and you will usually hear "not many." Then they look at the phone log and are surprised. You cannot fix a leak you have never measured, so start with the real number.

## What you need

- Your phone carrier or phone system call log for the last 30 days
- A spreadsheet, or pen and paper
- Twenty minutes

Most carriers and business phone systems let you download or view a call history from your online account. If you use a cell phone for the business, your phone's recent calls list works for a rough count.

## Step by step

1. **Pull 30 days of incoming calls.** Ignore outgoing calls.
2. **Mark the unanswered ones.** These are calls with no connection or a very short duration.
3. **Remove the noise.** Take out numbers you recognize as spam, vendors, or wrong numbers.
4. **Note the time of each missed call.** Pay attention to early mornings, evenings, weekends, and lunchtime.
5. **Check for repeat callers.** Someone who called twice in ten minutes was probably serious.
6. **Count how many you called back, and how fast.** Be honest here.

## What to look for

Add up the total and divide by four to get a weekly figure. Then ask:

- **Are most misses during working hours?** That points to being too busy to answer, which text-back and call answering solve.
- **Are most misses after hours?** That points to after-hours coverage, where emergency plumbing calls often come in.
- **How many were never called back?** These are leads you paid to attract and then lost.

## Turn it into dollars

Take the weekly number of real missed customer calls, multiply by roughly four for a month, then by the share of callers who would normally book, then by your average job value. [Our calculator does this for you](/#calculator), and you can change the assumptions to be as cautious as you like.

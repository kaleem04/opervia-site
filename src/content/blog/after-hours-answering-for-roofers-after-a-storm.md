A hailstorm or high wind can send a whole town looking for a roofer on the same evening. Calls arrive at night and all at once, and the first company to respond usually gets the inspection. The ones that go to voicemail do not get a second chance.

## Why storm weeks are hard on small crews

- Homeowners call several companies and pick whoever answers first.
- Your office, if you have one, is closed when most calls come in.
- Even a full-time office manager cannot answer fifty calls in an hour.
- Messages left overnight pile up, and by morning the best leads have booked someone else.

## What to have ready before the storm

**1. A clear after-hours message.** If a call goes unanswered, a text should follow within seconds that says who you are and asks for the address and a short description of the damage.

**2. A short list of questions.** Keep it to what you need to schedule an inspection: address, whether there is active water coming in, and whether they have already filed an insurance claim.

**3. Rules for true emergencies.** Water coming through the ceiling or a tree on the roof needs a faster response than a few missing shingles. Decide who gets paged and what a temporary tarp visit looks like.

**4. Open inspection slots.** Block out time on your calendar so bookings can land on real days instead of "someone will call you."

**5. A follow-up plan for quotes.** After the inspection, estimates on larger jobs often sit for weeks. A few polite check-ins keep you in the running without pestering anyone.

## A word on honesty

Do not promise what you cannot do. If your crew is booked out two weeks after a storm, say so and offer the first available inspection. Customers forgive a wait much more easily than a missed promise.
